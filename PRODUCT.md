# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The e-Square homepage serves several overlapping audiences from Marche-en-Famenne and the wider Famenne region, simultaneously:

- **Locals booking a space** — residents, associations, freelancers needing a meeting room, a coworking desk, the fablab, or an event venue. Their job: find availability, understand what's on offer, book quickly.
- **Entrepreneurs and makers exploring services** — people considering joining the community, taking part in formations, getting accompaniment on a project. Their job: assess whether e-Square fits their stage and ambition.
- **Event organizers, students, curious newcomers** — people who arrived from a flyer, a partner site, or word-of-mouth. Their job: understand what e-Square is in 10 seconds, then decide whether to dig deeper.

The homepage must serve all of these without choosing one and pushing the others to a side path. Visitors mix offline and online: they may have driven past the building, met someone from the team, or seen a poster before landing on the page.

## Product Purpose

e-Square is a physical place in Marche-en-Famenne: coworking, fablab, accompaniment, formations, events. The site exists to:

1. Make the place legible to people who haven't visited yet (what is this, who is it for, what happens here).
2. Convert intent into action for the audiences that already know what they want (book, sign up, contact).
3. Carry the identity of a specific, locally-rooted institution — not interchangeable with any other coworking brand.

Success looks like: a visitor who has never heard of e-Square can describe in one sentence what it is, who runs it, and what they'd come there for, within 30 seconds of arriving on the homepage.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, confident, locally-rooted.**

- **Warm** — the tone is welcoming and human, not corporate. Plain French, no jargon, no startup-speak. Photos of real people in the real building, not stock.
- **Confident** — the institution knows what it does and is good at it. Typography asserts, layout commits, copy makes claims. No hedging, no over-explaining, no "we're disrupting coworking."
- **Locally-rooted** — Marche-en-Famenne is the point, not a detail. Place names, real partners, Walloon-Belgian register where appropriate. The page should be impossible to imagine running anywhere else.

Voice: declarative, French-first, occasional Walloon-Belgian phrasing, never breathless.

## Anti-references

Specific things e-Square is **not**:

- **Generic SaaS / startup coworking** — no WeWork-style purple gradients, no hero-metric template ("3,000 members · 5 floors · 24/7 access"), no identical card grids, no "Join the future of work" copy.
- **Sterile corporate or municipal portal** — no bland public-sector layout with stock photos of diverse hands on a table, no top-heavy nav with twenty links, no condescending "Bienvenue sur le site officiel."
- Adjacent to avoid: trendy AI/crypto neon, glassmorphism, gradient text, clichéd "creative agency" maximalism with scroll-hijack and oversize cursors.

If the page looks like it could belong to any other coworking in Belgium with a logo swap, it has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Specific over generic.** Every block of content should be rooted in Marche-en-Famenne and in what e-Square actually does this month. No filler, no aspirational fluff, no "vibrant ecosystem of innovation."
2. **Civic warmth, not corporate cool.** The institution is a public-good actor, not a B2B brand. Posture: friendly, assured, accessible. Like a well-run library or a community-radio station, not like a Series-A startup.
3. **Earned, not borrowed.** Don't reach for SaaS templates, agency clichés, or AI-aesthetic reflexes. The identity should feel made for e-Square specifically.
4. **Mixed jobs, clear paths.** The homepage serves several audiences in parallel. Hierarchy should make each audience's next step legible without forcing one job to dominate the others.
5. **Editorial restraint.** Typography and whitespace carry identity. Decoration is rare and purposeful. Trust the reader; let the type do the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA** baseline across the public site. Belgian/EU public-good context makes this non-negotiable.
- **Contrast** ≥ 4.5:1 for body text, ≥ 3:1 for large text and UI components. Verify against the navy / cream / yellow palette in practice, not just theory.
- **Reduced motion** respected: any decorative animation gated on `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. Marquees, parallax, and scroll-driven effects must have a still fallback.
- **Keyboard navigation** complete on all interactive elements; visible focus rings (the skip-link pattern already in the explorations is the right shape).
- **French-first** content with clear, plain language. Avoid jargon and acronyms. If English appears, it's a deliberate exception.
- **Colorblind safety**: don't encode meaning in color alone. Yellow accent must always be paired with shape, weight, or text affordance.
